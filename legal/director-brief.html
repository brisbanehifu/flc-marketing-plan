Getting FLC licensed for retaining walls

What we need to do, what it costs, and what Alistair gets paid.

01

Where we stand

Any retaining wall job over $3,300 legally needs a QBCC licence. FLC doesn't have one. Alistair has one, but it's his personally — a personal licence doesn't cover the company.

To get FLC its own licence we have to employ Alistair as our licensed builder. That's the law, not a preference: a company licence requires a licensed builder who is a director, secretary or employee. A subcontractor doesn't count.

One thing to fix today

Our website, quotes and socials show “QBCC #76041”. That's Alistair's personal licence number, not ours. Advertising someone else's licence as the company's is a breach by itself — separate from everything below. Take it down now; it costs nothing and it stops the problem growing.

Until the licence is issued

We can't quote, sign or start any retaining wall over $3,300. Working unlicensed risks a fine around $43,000 — and worse, an unlicensed contractor can't legally be paid for the job.

His main jobTell us before we quote whether a wall is inside our licence and needs no engineer
What he doesn't doHe does not certify walls — an engineer does that
Why an hourly rate at all

The law says you must pay an employee for their time. So we pay him $70/hr as he works, then top him up to the full 5% when the job finishes. He gets 5%. The hourly rate is just paperwork that keeps us both safe.

05

One decision that's worth $34,000

Alistair's licence is a Contractor Licence, Builder — Low Rise, number 76041, expiring 16/03/27.

QBCC won't licence a company unless it holds a minimum amount of its own money, and how much depends on which licence we apply for:

If FLC's licence is…Money we must hold
Structural landscaping (trade)$12,000
A builder class, matching Alistair's$46,000
What we should do

Ask Alistair to add “Structural landscaping (trade)” to his own licence — it costs him $250–$500 — then FLC applies for that same licence and only needs $12,000 in the company instead of $46,000.

Confirm it with QBCC on the call before anyone lodges anything.

07

The money that has to sit in the company

QBCC won't licence a company unless it holds a minimum amount of its own money. For our size that's:

$12,000Must be in the company
$16k–$24kProfit to keep back to get there
$200,000Revenue cap at this level

It's more than $12,000 because tax takes a cut, and because we want a buffer — if the figure drops too far below what we told QBCC, we're forced into a paid accountant's report.

The trade-off to decide

Keeping profit back costs nothing but takes a quarter or two — and we can't take wall work in the meantime. Putting $12k–$20k in as a director loan or equity gets us licensed straight away. If there's wall work we want now, that's the better call.

08

What happens next

#Step
1Take licence 76041 off the website, quotes, email footers, socials and signage — today. It's Alistair's personal licence, not FLC's, and showing it as ours is a breach on its own.
2Diarise 16 March 2027 — Alistair's licence expires then. If it lapses we have no licensed builder.
3Ring QBCC on 139 333 — “Our nominee holds Contractor Licence 76041, Builder — Low Rise. Does that cover retaining walls, and can he nominate for a Structural landscaping (trade) company licence so we sit in SC1?” Also ask about home warranty insurance on standalone walls, and what counts toward the revenue cap.
4Build the $12,000 in the company, or decide to put it in.
5Get the employment agreement checked by a lawyer and signed — we can start this now, it isn't waiting on anything.
6Lodge the licence application — application form — then the nominee form naming Alistair.
7Set up payroll, WorkCover and QLeave before the first job.

Step 1 costs nothing and takes ten minutes. Steps 2 and 3 take an afternoon, and everything else depends on those answers.
